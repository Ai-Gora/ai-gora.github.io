# First-level administrative units in EU-27 countries

**Source:** Eurostat, *Regions in the European Union – Methodologies and working papers, NUTS 2024 edition* (KS-GQ-23-010), Table 5 "Correspondence between the NUTS levels and the national administrative and non-administrative units (based on NUTS 2024 and LAU 2022)". National sources replace Eurostat for France (Insee, 18 régions), Ireland (CSO, 31 local authorities), Poland (GUS, 16 województwa) and Slovenia (gov.si, 212 občine). Full row-by-row sources are in the article table `eu-administrative-divisions#first-level-table`.

**Method:** for each country, the chart counts the highest level that Eurostat Table 5 lists as an administrative unit (not marked with an asterisk). This choice is an editorial inference, not a Eurostat category.

**Licence:** Eurostat data may be reused with acknowledgement of the source (Eurostat copyright notice, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/main/help/copyright-notice). The chart data file is a derived count table.

**Long description:** horizontal bar chart with 27 bars, one per EU member state, sorted from most to fewest units. The x-axis uses a log scale because the counts span two orders of magnitude. Cyprus has the most units (615 municipalities and communities). Slovenia has 212 municipalities and Luxembourg 102 communes. Most countries have between 8 and 43 units. Belgium and Portugal have the fewest, with 3 each.
