Human anatomy is the scientific study of the body's structures.[^1]
This article covers the basic vocabulary: levels of organisation, tissues, organ systems, the anatomical position, body planes, body cavities and the skeleton.

## Anatomy and physiology {#what-anatomy-is}

Anatomy studies the structures of the body.[^1]
Physiology studies how those structures work and support the functions of life.[^1]
Some structures are so small that they can only be studied with a microscope.[^1]

## Levels of organisation {#levels}

The body is often described in six levels of increasing complexity.[^2]
The levels are chemical, cellular, tissue, organ, organ system and organism.[^2]
An organ is a structure made of two or more types of tissue.[^2]
An organ system is a group of organs that work together.[^2]
The organism, a whole living person, is the highest level.[^2]

## Tissues {#tissues}

A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together as a unit.[^5]
The body has four main tissue types: epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous tissue.[^5]
For example, the wall and lining of the stomach contain all four types.[^4]

## Organ systems {#organ-systems}

Textbooks do not all count organ systems the same way.[^2][^4]
The U.S. National Cancer Institute training course lists ten major systems: skeletal, muscular, nervous, endocrine, cardiovascular, lymphatic, respiratory, digestive, urinary and reproductive.[^4]
The OpenStax textbook counts eleven organ systems.[^2]
One of its additions is the integumentary system: the skin, hair and nails.[^2]

## Anatomical position {#anatomical-position}

The anatomical position is the standard reference posture of the body.[^3]
The body stands upright, with the feet parallel and the toes pointing forward.[^3]
The arms are held out to the sides, and the palms face forward.[^3]
Directional terms such as "anterior" are always used as if the body were in this position.[^3]

## Body planes {#planes}

A sagittal plane is vertical and runs from front to back.[^6]
It divides the body into right and left parts.[^6]
The median plane is the sagittal plane through the midline, and it gives two equal halves.[^6]
The coronal plane, also called the frontal plane, is vertical and runs from side to side.[^6]
It divides the body into a front (anterior) part and a back (posterior) part.[^6]
The transverse plane, also called the axial plane, is horizontal.[^6]
It divides the body into upper and lower parts.[^6]

## Body cavities {#cavities}

The two main body cavities are the ventral cavity and the dorsal cavity.[^6]
The ventral cavity is the larger of the two.[^6]
The diaphragm, a dome-shaped breathing muscle, divides it into the thoracic cavity and the abdominopelvic cavity.[^6]
The thoracic cavity holds the heart, the lungs, the trachea and the oesophagus.[^6]
The abdominal part of the abdominopelvic cavity holds most of the digestive tract and the kidneys.[^6]
The pelvic part holds most of the urinary and reproductive organs and the rectum.[^6]
The dorsal cavity has two parts: the cranial cavity, which holds the brain, and the vertebral canal, which holds the spinal cord.[^6]

## Skeleton {#skeleton}

The adult skeleton usually has 206 named bones.[^7]
The axial skeleton has 80 bones: the bones of the head, the vertebral column, the ribs and the sternum.[^7]
The appendicular skeleton has 126 bones: the limbs and the girdles that attach them to the axial skeleton.[^7]

## Key claims {#key-claims}

- Anatomy studies the body's structures; physiology studies how they work.[^1]
- The body is described in six levels, from chemical to organism.[^2]
- There are four main tissue types: epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous.[^5]
- Sources differ on the number of organ systems: ten in the NCI course, eleven in OpenStax.[^2][^4]
- In the anatomical position the body stands upright with the palms facing forward.[^3]
- The adult skeleton usually has 206 bones: 80 axial and 126 appendicular.[^7]
