In this article, a first-level administrative division means the largest unit with its own administration directly below the national level.
Every EU country sets these units itself, while the EU's NUTS classification adds a common statistical layer alongside them.[^1][^2]

## Administrative units and statistical regions {#definition}

The EU developed NUTS to refer to the regions of its member countries for statistical purposes.[^1]
NUTS generally mirrors each country's administrative divisions, so that data are easy to collect and policies easy to apply.[^2]
Some NUTS levels match administrative units, while others are non-administrative and exist only for statistics.[^2]
The two systems therefore often overlap, but they are not the same thing.[^2]

## NUTS regions {#nuts}

NUTS stands for Nomenclature of territorial units for statistics.[^1]
It divides each EU country into three levels.[^1]
NUTS 1 regions are major socio-economic regions.[^1]
NUTS 2 regions are basic regions for regional policies.[^1]
NUTS 3 regions are small regions for specific diagnoses.[^1]
The NUTS 2024 classification, valid from 1 January 2024, has 92 NUTS 1, 244 NUTS 2 and 1,165 NUTS 3 regions.[^1]
The NUTS 2027 classification, valid from 1 January 2027, has 91 NUTS 1, 242 NUTS 2 and 1,170 NUTS 3 regions.[^1]
Regions eligible for EU cohesion policy support are defined at NUTS 2 level.[^1]

## How NUTS regions are drawn {#principles}

Each NUTS level has a population range.[^2]
NUTS 1 regions should have 3,000,000 to 7,000,000 inhabitants.[^2]
NUTS 2 regions should have 800,000 to 3,000,000 inhabitants.[^2]
NUTS 3 regions should have 150,000 to 800,000 inhabitants.[^2]
Where a NUTS level matches an administrative level, only the average population of its regions has to fall within the range.[^2]
The classification is generally changed no more often than every 3 years.[^2]

## Local administrative units {#lau}

Below NUTS 3, Eurostat keeps a list of local administrative units (LAU).[^3]
LAUs are the EU's municipalities and communes, and they are the building blocks of NUTS.[^3]
Since 2017 there has been only one LAU level.[^3]
EU countries send Eurostat an updated list of their LAUs every year.[^3]

## Examples {#examples}

Germany is a federal state made up of 16 Länder.[^4]
Three of them, Berlin, Hamburg and Bremen, are city-states.[^4]
France has 18 regions: 13 in metropolitan France and 5 overseas (Guadeloupe, Martinique, Guyane, La Réunion and Mayotte).[^5]
These 18 regions contain 101 départements.[^5]
On 1 January 2025 Poland had 16 voivodeships (*województwa*), 314 counties (*powiaty*), 66 cities with county status and 2,479 municipalities (*gminy*).[^6]

## Slovenia {#slovenia}

Slovenia has 212 municipalities (*občine*), and 12 of them have the status of urban municipality.[^7]
The constitution also provides for provinces (*pokrajine*), but they have not been established.[^7]
Municipalities are therefore the only level of local self-government.[^7]
Slovenia also has 58 administrative units (*upravne enote*), which are local offices of the state administration rather than self-governing bodies.[^10]
For statistics, the national classification groups the 212 municipalities into 12 statistical regions and 2 cohesion regions.[^8]
Since 1 January 2008 the two cohesion regions, Eastern Slovenia (*Vzhodna Slovenija*) and Western Slovenia (*Zahodna Slovenija*), form Slovenia's NUTS 2 level.[^9]

## Key claims {#key-claims}

- NUTS divides each EU country into three levels of statistical regions.[^1]
- NUTS 2024 has 92 NUTS 1, 244 NUTS 2 and 1,165 NUTS 3 regions.[^1]
- NUTS generally follows national administrative divisions.[^2]
- Germany has 16 Länder, France 18 regions and Poland 16 voivodeships.[^4][^5][^6]
- Slovenia has 212 municipalities and no provinces.[^7]
- Slovenia's 12 statistical regions and 2 cohesion regions are statistical units, not administrations.[^8][^9]
